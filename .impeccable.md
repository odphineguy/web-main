# Design Context — Abe Media

This file captures the design context for the Abe Media marketing site. It guides future audits, critiques, new features, and redesigns. If the brand direction shifts, update this file first.

## Design Context

### Users

**Primary audience:** Owners and operators of plumbing, HVAC, moving, junk-removal, landscaping, logistics, and other service businesses in English- and Spanish-speaking U.S. markets. Many have been underserved by generic local agencies and enterprise software that is too cold, too expensive, or disconnected from how their operation actually runs.

**Context of use:** They are evaluating a meaningful investment in AI phone agents, dispatch software, workflow automation, or a new digital product. They may be comparison-shopping on mobile during off-hours and need to know quickly that Abe Media is real, experienced, and capable of handling operational complexity.

**Job to be done:** Understand what Abe Media builds, see credible proof that it works, and decide whether to schedule a call. The site should make a business owner feel that Abe Media understands the operation behind the software—not just the technology.

### Brand Personality

**Three words:** Bold. Operational. Human.

**Voice and tone:**
- Enterprise-grade outcomes delivered with the directness of an experienced operator.
- Numbers, outcomes, and specifics over empty adjectives.
- Bilingual as an intrinsic capability, not a secondary feature badge.
- Calm confidence—never pushy, needy, or generic.
- Founder experience is proof: the tools are built by someone who ran dispatch before automating it.

**First-impression emotional goal:** *“This looks different—and they understand how my business actually works.”*

**Anti-personality:**
- Not a playful “team of creatives” agency.
- Not dev-bro technical or cold enterprise SaaS.
- Not a generic local web-design template.
- Not a conventional hero + stats + card grid + testimonial landing page.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Core direction:** A bold editorial homepage with a light-mode foundation, selective dark chapters, cinematic media, compressed display typography, Abe Media orange accents, and purposeful scroll choreography.

**Positive references:**
- Editorial whitespace and confident restraint from Breef and Addison Whitney.
- Cinematic grayscale video, strong typography, alternating light/dark sections, and interactive footer energy from Octopush.
- The original Abe Media mock’s oversized industrial type, operational side rails, and unexpected page rhythm.

**Typography:** Distinctive condensed display type for major statements, paired with a refined and highly readable body face. Headlines should be wide and concise rather than forming unnecessarily tall stacks.

**Color:** Warm off-white and tinted dark neutrals with orange used selectively for meaningful words, active states, and calls to action. Orange is the brand signal, not the entire design.

**Motion:** Scroll effects should tell the operational story—movement, handoffs, transformation, and progress. Prefer a few memorable sequences over decorative movement on every element. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Design Principles

1. **Typography carries the identity.** Use scale, proportion, and selective orange words to make statements memorable without turning them into towering text walls.
2. **Show the operation, not generic technology.** Every section should connect software to a real workflow, exception, handoff, or business outcome.
3. **Use wide, asymmetric compositions.** Vary section rhythm and avoid repetitive centered layouts or identical card grids.
4. **Whitespace is a capability signal.** Give major ideas room, but do not create empty vertical space without purpose.
5. **Motion is storytelling.** Scroll behavior should reveal cause, progress, or transformation while keeping the content readable and accessible.
6. **Bilingual parity.** English and Spanish should both feel intentionally written and designed.
7. **Preserve proof.** The visual-estimate before/after experience, real builds, and founder operating history are core credibility elements.
